# Plan: precise collision, falling, and real jumps

Status: agreed (2026-09-29), with pathfinding moved to a navmesh; not started.

## Why

Collision today is a 1 m grid, and characters are points on it:

- `ChunkObstacles` marks a 64 × 64 grid of cells per chunk as blocked. Walls and buildings come from the bake's
  `structure_cells`; trees and boulders block every cell whose centre is within their radius; water blocks when it's too
  deep; slopes block when any cell's corners differ by more than `max_step` (1.2 m).
- `find_path` runs A* over those cells, then string-pulls the path by sampling the line every 0.25 m.
- `step` moves a point. When it's steered, it slides along one axis if the next cell is blocked.
- Heights are never simulated: a character's height is always the ground under it.

The grid is fine for terrain, but walls, fences and buildings are thin and can lie at any angle. A 0.5 m fence blocks a
whole metre of cells, so you stop up to a metre short of it. A slanting wall blocks a staircase of cells. A 1.5 m doorway
is either a whole cell or nothing. Characters have no width, so they clip into the edges of walls.

The slope rule has no direction, so a steep cell blocks both ways and you can't go down a slope that you couldn't climb.

Jumping is only an animation: `Gait::jump` lifts the model 0.45 m, and the server only records `LastJump`.

## The idea

Each character becomes a capsule that moves through real collision shapes. We use
[Rapier](https://rapier.rs)'s kinematic character controller, which already does the parts that are easy to get
subtly wrong:

- sliding along surfaces;
- a maximum slope it can climb;
- sliding down slopes that are too steep;
- stepping up small ledges;
- snapping to the ground on the way down.

We don't run a physics simulation. There are no rigid bodies to integrate: the controller only asks the query pipeline
what is in the way. This keeps it cheap and predictable. Rapier has no Bevy dependency, builds for wasm, and has an
`enhanced-determinism` feature. It's the same crate that `bevy_rapier` wraps; we use it directly, so the server and the
client share one plain-Rust implementation.

Height becomes simulated state: `(x, y, height)` plus a vertical speed. Gravity pulls characters down whatever they can't
stand on. That gives falling down steep slopes and cliffs for free, and turns jumps into real jumps.

Pathfinding moves to a navmesh. The grid's routes are poor, and bridges are coming soon, which a grid can't
express at all. Navmesh tiles are generated by [rerecast](https://github.com/janhohenheim/rerecast), a Rust port of
Recast, from the same geometry as the colliders. Routes are found on them by [landmass](https://crates.io/crates/landmass).
See "Pathfinding" below.

## Shapes

A new crate, `venture_collision`, turns world data into Rapier colliders. It has no Bevy dependency. It sits beside
`venture_navigation`, and `venture_simulation` uses it.

| Source | Collider |
|---|---|
| Terrain (per chunk) | `HeightField`, 64 × 64 cells at 1 m (the chunk's corner heights). |
| Deep water and lava | No collider. Movement rules keep characters out, as they do today, via a per-chunk "no entry" cell mask. |
| Dry-stone wall, fence, hedge, curtain | A convex box per segment between baked samples. Its width is `half_width × 2`, its height is the wall's height, and its bottom follows the baked ground heights. The segments overlap at joints, so there are no seams. |
| Building walls | A box per outer-wall face and per inner wall, from the same `Frame`/`Footprint` as the generator (scaled, anticlockwise), split at doorways. Floors are the levelled ground, so they need nothing extra. |
| Trees | A capsule for the trunk. Its radius is today's `blocking_radius × scale`, and its height is the trunk's height. Bushes get none. Felled trees are removed when woodcutting changes. |
| Dead trees, boulders | A capsule or a ball of today's radius. |

The character is a capsule: 0.3 m radius and 1.8 m tall.

Colliders are built per chunk and kept in a map keyed by `ChunkPos`, with one `QueryPipeline` over everything loaded:

- **On the client**, chunks are added and removed as they stream in, next to `ObstacleStore`.
- **On the server**, colliders are built only for chunks within one chunk of a player, NPC or monster that is moving.
  They are built from the chunk files on demand, then dropped after a while unused. A full 4096 m world as heightfields
  would be about 70 MB, so building everything up front isn't worth it.

A world reload (`WorldChanged`) drops the changed chunks' colliders, as streaming already does.

## Movement

`venture_simulation::movement::step` becomes a thin wrapper round `KinematicCharacterController::move_shape`. It runs
on the server, which decides, and on the client, which predicts. The settings go in `server.ron`, so they can be tuned
without code:

| Setting | Proposed value | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `max_slope_climb_angle` | ≈ 50° | Matches today's 1.2 m per 1 m. You can't walk up anything steeper. |
| `min_slope_slide_angle` | ≈ 50° | Anything steeper slides you down under gravity. |
| `autostep` | 0.4 m high, 0.3 m wide, not on dynamic bodies | Kerbs, plinths and doorsteps. |
| `snap_to_ground` | 0.5 m | Running downhill sticks to the ground instead of hopping. |
| `offset` | 0.02 m | Gap kept from surfaces. |
| gravity | 20 m/s² | Snappier than 9.8, as most games are. |

Each tick, the desired translation is the horizontal run towards the next waypoint (or the steering direction), plus
the vertical speed × dt. The controller returns the movement it actually made and whether the character is grounded.
When grounded, the vertical speed resets.

- **Steep slopes:** you can't climb them, because the climb angle stops you. You can go down them, because gravity
  plus sliding takes you down. Characters don't take fall damage in this plan; that could come later.
- **Water:** stays a rule, not a collider. A step that would end in a "no entry" cell is refused, as today.
- **Routes:** following a route still drops waypoints as they're reached. When the controller is blocked for about a
  second, for example by another character's route or a tree that has just grown back, the route is re-planned.

Characters don't collide with each other, as today.

## Jumping

The jump becomes a vertical speed set by the server when it accepts `ClientMessage::Jump`, and only while grounded. It
is still rate-limited by `LastJump`. `jump_height` goes in `server.ron`, and the initial speed is
`sqrt(2 × g × jump_height)`.

`jump_height` is **1.4 m**. At that height you clear fences (1.2 m) and dry-stone walls (1.3 m), but not hedges (1.7 m),
buildings or curtain walls. At g = 20 a jump is about 0.75 s in the air. Today's lift is 0.45 m.

The client predicts the jump at once, as it does now. `Gait` takes its lift from the simulated height instead of its own
parabola, so the animation and the capsule agree. The jump animation plays while the character isn't grounded.
Landing could get a short blend later.

## Protocol

Positions in snapshots gain a height, becoming `(x, y, height)`. Reconciliation compares all three. Remote characters
are interpolated with their height, so others are seen jumping over walls and falling off cliffs. This bumps the
protocol version to 32.

## Pathfinding: a navmesh

The grid's A* is replaced by a navigation mesh: convex polygons covering everywhere a character can stand, with
routes found over polygons instead of cells. It is the standard in Unreal, Unity and Godot, and it suits us well:

- it has far fewer nodes to search than a grid, and routes are any-angle;
- its edges follow walls, doorways and banks exactly, less the character's radius;
- it handles overlapping levels, such as a bridge over a road or a river, which a grid can't express at all.

### Generating it: rerecast

[rerecast](https://github.com/janhohenheim/rerecast) (0.4) is a Rust port of Recast. Its core crate doesn't need
Bevy, is `no_std`, and has a `libm` feature for results that are the same on every platform. It supports tiles,
and it produces the two usual meshes:

- a **polygon mesh**, for searching;
- a **detail mesh**, for the heights.

Recast voxelises triangles, so its input is a triangle soup, built by `venture_collision` from the same shapes as the
colliders:

- the terrain;
- walls, as their boxes;
- buildings' walls, floors and (later) stairs;
- tree trunks and boulders.

Recast marks what's walkable using the same settings as the controller:

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Agent radius | 0.3 m |
| Agent height | 1.8 m |
| Walkable slope | 50° |
| Walkable climb | 0.4 m |
| Cell size | 0.15 m (about half the agent radius, as Recast recommends) |
| Cell height | 0.1 m |

- **Deep water and lava:** marked unwalkable, as Recast's convex "area" volumes.
- **Shallow water:** marked with its own area type, so routes cost more through it and avoid wading when there's a dry
  way round.

**One tile per chunk (64 m).** Each tile is generated with Recast's usual border of a few cells, so neighbouring
tiles' edges line up. Tiles are generated **at bake time** and stored beside each chunk's file (`chunks/x_y.nav`).
That adds to the bake, but the bake is already incremental: only chunks whose terrain, walls or buildings changed
are regenerated. Trees change during play, so the tiles leave them out. The controller already slides characters
round trunks, and the path follower re-plans if one gets stuck (see "Trees", below).

### Searching it: landmass

[landmass](https://crates.io/crates/landmass) (0.9) has no Bevy dependency. It takes:

- an **archipelago** of **islands**, each holding one navmesh;
- optional **height meshes**, which Recast's detail mesh maps onto;
- **polygon type costs**, which is how routes avoid shallow water.

It gives us:

- `find_path` for one-off queries;
- path simplification;
- **agents**, which steer along paths and **avoid each other**;
- **animation links**: one-way off-mesh connections, for drops and, later, jumps.

landmass_rerecast is Bevy-only, so we write the small conversion from Recast's meshes ourselves: polygons and
vertices to a `NavigationMesh`, and the detail triangles to its `HeightNavigationMesh`.

Each chunk tile is one island in a shared `Archipelago<XYZ>`. Islands are added and removed as chunks load on the
server, and as they stream in on the client.

**Joining the tiles is the first thing to prove.** landmass has to link one island's boundary edges to the next
island's. The fallback is [polyanya](https://crates.io/crates/polyanya) (0.17, no Bevy dependency), which also has
layers for overlapping levels and one-way layers.

**What uses it:**

- **Players' click-to-move** uses `find_path` on the server. The client runs the same query for its prediction, which
  replaces `plan_route`.
- **NPCs and monsters** become landmass agents. Their desired velocity feeds the character controller. They stop
  stacking on each other, and they walk around players, who are landmass characters.
- **Picking a spot to stand** (`open_ground_near`, spawns, NPC homes) uses landmass's `sample_point`, so every spot
  that's picked is on the navmesh.

### Going down: drop links

Recast's climb limit applies both ways, so a bank too steep to climb is a gap in the navmesh either way. So that
routes can go down things you can't climb up, the bake adds one-way **drop links** (landmass animation links):

- A drop link is found from each navmesh boundary edge that has walkable navmesh below it. The drop must be within 6 m
  down and 1.5 m out.
- A route may take a drop link, and the character controller falls down it naturally.

This is how Unreal's "jump down" links work.

**Jump links over fences and walls** are the same mechanism, going over rather than down. They come later if you want
click-to-move to hop fences; steering and Space can jump them from step 5.

### Trees

Trees are left out of the tiles, so felling and regrowth never touch the navmesh:

- A trunk is 0.3–0.6 m across, and the controller slides round it.
- If a character makes no progress for about a second, the path follower re-plans. The planner goes round the stuck
  character's current position, and landmass agents' avoidance also pushes them round.

If that looks poor in dense woods, trunks can go in the tiles. Then felling and regrowth regenerate one tile in the
background: a 64 m Recast tile takes tens of milliseconds.

## Steps

Each step is committed and tested on its own.

1. **`venture_collision`: shapes.** Build the colliders from the chunk, walls, buildings and props, plus the same shapes
   as triangles, for Recast. Tests: a fence blocks within 0.3 m of its face (not a whole cell); doorways are open; a
   tree's trunk blocks at its radius.
2. **Movement with the controller.** Height and vertical speed go in `venture_simulation`, and `step` uses
   `move_shape`, with its settings in `server.ron`. Tests: sliding along a slanting wall; stopped at the foot of a steep
   bank but sliding down it from the top; stepping onto a 0.3 m ledge; no tunnelling at high speed.
3. **Server.** Chunk colliders load on demand around characters, and reload with the world. Players, NPCs and monsters
   move with them.
4. **Protocol and client.** Height goes in snapshots (protocol version 32). Prediction uses the same `step`, and
   reconciliation and interpolation include height. The client builds colliders as chunks stream in.
5. **Jumping.** A jump speed from `jump_height` (1.4 m, in `server.ron`), set on the server and predicted on the client.
   `Gait` follows the simulated height.
6. **Navmesh tiles.** rerecast in the bake: one tile per chunk, stored beside the chunk, and regenerated incrementally,
   with drop links. Plus the conversion to landmass, and a proof that neighbouring islands join up (polyanya is the
   fallback).
7. **Routes on the navmesh.** `find_path` for players on the server and the client, landmass agents for NPCs and monsters,
   and `sample_point` for picking spots. Shallow water gets a higher cost. Profile it with many agents.
8. **Clean up.** Remove `structure_cells`, `ChunkObstacles`' slope rule and the grid A*. The chunk format becomes 5, and the
   world is rebaked. Update `world-model.md`, `client-server.md`, `architecture.md` and `building-and-running.md`.

Steps 1–5 give close contact with walls, falling and jumping. Click-to-move stays on the 1 m grid until 6–7, which
make routes good and ready for bridges.

## Risks

- **Prediction drift.** The client and the server run the same code on the same data. The `enhanced-determinism` feature
  (Rapier) and `libm` feature (rerecast) cover platform differences in floating point, and the snap-back threshold covers
  what's left.
- **Server cost.** A character costs a few shape-casts per tick, and idle characters skip the controller. Landmass's
  `find_path` searches polygons, not cells, so it should be much cheaper than today's A*.
- **Bake time.** About 4,000 tiles at tens of milliseconds each is a couple of minutes across threads, for a full bake only.
- **Young crates.** rerecast is marked unstable, and landmass and Rapier change their APIs between versions. Versions are
  pinned, and each crate is used from one place: `venture_collision` for Rapier and the triangles, `venture_navigation`
  for rerecast and landmass.
